Hostapd: PACKAGECONFIG is the wrong question for this recipe

  bitbake -e hostapd | grep -n PACKAGECONFIG

  returned eighteen matching lines and **not one of them was a
  PACKAGECONFIG= assignment.** What it did show:

    # $PACKAGECONFIG [2 operations]
    # $PACKAGECONFIG_CONFARGS
    PACKAGECONFIG_CONFARGS=""

  An empty CONFARGS is the fact that settles it: no feature in this recipe
  reaches the build through a configure argument. Compare mosquitto on the
  same day, where CONFARGS carried WITH_TLS=ON and seven more.

  The mechanism is a kernel-style configuration file instead:

    bitbake -e hostapd | grep -n ^SRC_URI

    SRC_URI=" http://w1.fi/releases/hostapd-2.10.tar.gz
              file://defconfig
              file://init
              file://hostapd.service
              ... 17 patch files ...  "

  file://defconfig is the answer. hostapd is configured by the symbols in
  that file, the way a kernel is, and the recipe installs it as .config.

  So question 1 asked hostapd the wrong thing, by analogy with mosquitto.
  It is not a defect in the question: both halves were written before
  either could be read, and the analogy was reasonable until the first
  reading broke it. The restated question is in this directory's README.

What the pinned revision carries, which matters for this project

  hostapd 2.10 upstream plus 17 patch files. Four distinct CVEs:

    CVE-2022-37660
    CVE-2023-52160
    CVE-2024-3596
    CVE-2025-24912

  and two hardening patches for SAE rejected-groups handling that carry no
  CVE number.

  Recorded because this project's subject is a security boundary. The PKI
  work protects the broker; hostapd is the boundary itself, and what it has
  been patched against is a property of the pinned layer revision rather
  than of the version number 2.10. A reader who sees "hostapd 2.10" and
  stops there would draw the wrong conclusion about its age.
