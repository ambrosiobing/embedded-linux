WHAT CHANGED SINCE THURSDAY, AND WHY THE FIRST TWO CAPTURES WERE VOID

Thursday's captures were taken with nothing bound to 0x6b, so the part was
free-running and a raw burst read returned live data. The overlay now
declares it, st_lsm6dsx owns it, and THE DRIVER KEEPS THE PART IN POWER-DOWN
WHEN NO BUFFER IS ENABLED, powering it up only for the duration of a sysfs
read.

So i2ctransfer first refused the address outright:

    Error: Could not set address to 0x6b: Device or resource busy

and with -f, which skips the claimed-address check but not the bus lock, it
returned a frozen register. The symptom was not an error. It was two
captures scoring identically to four decimal places:

    flat.txt    X -0.0026  Y +0.0101  Z +1.0070 g   roll +0.877 pitch +0.229
    roll90.txt  X -0.0026  Y +0.0101  Z +1.0070 g   roll +0.877 pitch +0.229

A tilted board and a flat board cannot agree like that. The register was
confirmed directly:

    CTRL1 before: 0x00        power-down
    i2cset -f -y 1 0x6b 0x10 0x06
    CTRL1 after:  0x06        120 Hz

after which five spaced reads all differed. 0x06 is 120 Hz on this part's
ladder, which is also where iio-stream's rate policy lands and what ST's own
fusion sketch uses.

MAKING THE DRIVER BIND CORRECTLY BROKE THE CAPTURE METHOD THAT HAD WORKED
ALL WEEK, and the failure was silent. Anything that returns the part to the
driver, a sampling_frequency write, an in_accel_*_raw read, or enabling a
buffer, can put it back to sleep. Raw i2ctransfer reads leave CTRL1 alone.

THE LEVEL POSE, WHICH IS MET

    mean acceleration  X -0.0031  Y +0.0107  Z +1.0068 g
    magnitude          1.0069 g
    filter   roll    +0.348 deg   pitch   +0.283 deg
    direct   roll    +0.607 deg   pitch   +0.174 deg
    orientation error  0.281 deg final

Both inside the 2 degree tolerance, on data that moves. The magnitude of
1.0069 rather than 1.0000 is this part's own scale accuracy and it appears at
every pose, so 1.007 is the bench reference against which a pose that moved
reads high.

One attempt before this read pitch -5.369 with the two routes agreeing to
0.010 degrees. That was not a measurement problem: the board was sitting 5.4
degrees off level on its own cables. The filter reporting the fixture
correctly is the filter working.

Pitch was never the problem: +0.294, -0.078, +0.110 across the attempts.

A CLAIM MADE AND WITHDRAWN WITHIN TWO MEASUREMENTS

Attempt 1 showed the filter at +92.795 and the trigonometry at +95.266, and
re-running the same capture with the settling window doubled changed neither.
That was written up here as a steady-state bias in the filter, with the
observation that scoring the filter alone would pass a pose that was out of
tolerance by under-reporting it.

Attempts 2 and 3 refute it: at a square pose the two routes agree to 0.21
degrees, as they do flat. The gap belonged to attempt 1, not to the filter,
and generalising it from one pose was wrong. What survives is the method
rather than the conclusion: the orientation error between the two routes is
the number that says whether a pose is worth scoring, and 2.471 should have
been read as "this capture is suspect" rather than as "the filter is biased".

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE CRITERION

The level clause is met. The ninety clause needs a square reference this
bench does not have: a wall, a machinist square, or a shim, rather than
anything standing on the bench, which leans. Three attempts converged on 3.3
degrees from two different holds, so the limit is the fixture and not the
hand, and it is 0.3 degrees from the tolerance.

That is a number to decide on rather than an argument to continue. Either a
square is found, or the tolerance is restated at 3.5 degrees with this
measurement as the reason. Both are defensible and neither is a measurement
problem.
