Acceptance criterion 8: the AHRS against real gravity
=====================================================

Thursday 1 October 2026. The filter has been checked against synthetic
rotations since 16 September 2026 and had never seen a real measurement,
because ahrs.py had no way to read one. It has one now: --capture.

    python3 ahrs.py --capture FILE --scale 16384 --rate 126.4

THE GYROSCOPE IS ZERO ON EVERY UPDATE, and that is a choice rather than a
gap. These captures carry the accelerometer alone, and with no angular
rate the Madgwick update reduces to its accelerometer correction, which is
exactly the path a static orientation check should exercise. It would be
the wrong instrument for a moving test.

LEVEL, from the quiet capture of Wednesday 30 September 2026
------------------------------------------------------------

    mean acceleration  X -0.0053  Y +0.0135  Z +1.0067 g
    magnitude          1.0068 g
    samples            4000 used, 0 damaged

    filter   roll  +0.770 deg   pitch +0.302 deg
    direct   roll  +0.770 deg   pitch +0.302 deg
    gap            0.000 deg          0.000 deg

PASS against the criterion's first half: both within 2 degrees of zero.

The bench itself is 0.83 degrees off level, which is the first time that
has been measured rather than assumed.

THE CROSS-CHECK IS THE POINT, not the pass. "direct" is roll and pitch
computed straight from the mean gravity vector by trigonometry, with no
filter involved at all. The two routes use the same samples and share no
code, so agreement to a thousandth of a degree says the filter's sign
conventions and frame handedness are right on real data. That is the
property the project's own notes say published Madgwick implementations
most often get wrong, and it had never been tested here.

BETA SETS THE NOISE AND DOES NOT BIAS THE ANSWER
------------------------------------------------

    beta    step beta*dt    roll spread    pitch spread    gap
    0.5       0.227 deg      0.767 deg      0.762 deg     0.006 deg
    0.1       0.045 deg      0.252 deg      0.255 deg     0.000 deg
    0.02      0.009 deg      0.095 deg      0.096 deg     0.001 deg

Twenty-five times the gain costs eight times the output spread and moves
the answer by six thousandths of a degree. The spread falls more slowly
than the gain, roughly as its square root, which is what a low-pass whose
bandwidth follows beta does to white input noise.

So the classic Madgwick trade is measured on this bench rather than
asserted: beta buys convergence speed and is paid for in output noise, and
it does not buy or cost accuracy.

At beta 0.02 the spread is 0.095 degrees. The sensor's own 0.36 mg noise on
a one g vector is 0.02 degrees, so the filter is still five times above
what the accelerometer alone would allow, and lowering beta further would
close some of that at the cost of a slower response.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN
------------------

The criterion's second half: within 3 degrees after a 90 degree rotation
about each axis. That needs two captures that do not exist yet, with the
board turned onto each edge. Nothing in software stands in the way.
