Acceptance criterion 8: the AHRS against real gravity
=====================================================

Thursday 1 October 2026. The filter has been checked against synthetic
rotations since 16 September 2026 and had never seen a real measurement,
because ahrs.py had no way to read one. It has one now: --capture.

    python3 ahrs.py --capture FILE --scale 16384 --rate 126.4

THE GYROSCOPE IS ZERO ON EVERY UPDATE, and that is a choice rather than a
gap. These captures carry the accelerometer alone, and with no angular
rate the Madgwick update reduces to its accelerometer correction, which is
exactly the path a static orientation check should exercise. It would be
the wrong instrument for a moving test.

LEVEL, from the quiet capture of Wednesday 30 September 2026
------------------------------------------------------------

    mean acceleration  X -0.0053  Y +0.0135  Z +1.0067 g
    magnitude          1.0068 g
    samples            4000 used, 0 damaged

    filter   roll  +0.770 deg   pitch +0.302 deg
    direct   roll  +0.770 deg   pitch +0.302 deg
    gap            0.000 deg          0.000 deg

PASS against the criterion's first half: both within 2 degrees of zero.

The bench itself is 0.83 degrees off level, which is the first time that
has been measured rather than assumed.

THE CROSS-CHECK IS THE POINT, not the pass. "direct" is roll and pitch
computed straight from the mean gravity vector by trigonometry, with no
filter involved at all. The two routes use the same samples and share no
code, so agreement to a thousandth of a degree says the filter's sign
conventions and frame handedness are right on real data. That is the
property the project's own notes say published Madgwick implementations
most often get wrong, and it had never been tested here.

BETA SETS THE NOISE AND DOES NOT BIAS THE ANSWER
------------------------------------------------

    beta    step beta*dt    roll spread    pitch spread    gap
    0.5       0.227 deg      0.767 deg      0.762 deg     0.006 deg
    0.1       0.045 deg      0.252 deg      0.255 deg     0.000 deg
    0.02      0.009 deg      0.095 deg      0.096 deg     0.001 deg

Twenty-five times the gain costs eight times the output spread and moves
the answer by six thousandths of a degree. The spread falls more slowly
than the gain, roughly as its square root, which is what a low-pass whose
bandwidth follows beta does to white input noise.

So the classic Madgwick trade is measured on this bench rather than
asserted: beta buys convergence speed and is paid for in output noise, and
it does not buy or cost accuracy.

At beta 0.02 the spread is 0.095 degrees. The sensor's own 0.36 mg noise on
a one g vector is 0.02 degrees, so the filter is still five times above
what the accelerometer alone would allow, and lowering beta further would
close some of that at the cost of a slower response.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN
------------------

The criterion's second half: within 3 degrees after a 90 degree rotation
about each axis. That needs two captures that do not exist yet, with the
board turned onto each edge. Nothing in software stands in the way.

THE NINETY DEGREE POSES, and a defect in the criterion
======================================================

Four captures, Thursday 1 October 2026, 2000 samples each at 126 Hz.

  file          measured mean              magnitude   off the intended axis
  roll90        Y +1.0025, Z -0.0771 g      1.0055 g        4.4 deg
  roll90b       Y +1.0055, Z -0.0299 g      1.0059 g        2.2 deg
  pitch90       X +0.9738, Y +0.1076 g      0.9960 g       12.1 deg
  pitch90b      X +0.9919, Z -0.1004 g      0.9970 g        6.8 deg

Magnitude stays between 0.996 and 1.007 g in every pose, so no axis is
swapped or inverted and the board was still enough to be read.

WHAT THE FILTER DID, against trigonometry on the same samples:

  file                      converged           orientation error
  flat                      roll +0.77, pitch +0.32     0.015 deg
  roll90                    roll +94.40, pitch +0.10    0.091 deg
  roll90b                   roll +91.33, pitch +0.32    0.381 deg
  pitch90                   roll +148.73, pitch -77.93  0.072 deg
  pitch90b                  roll +177.24, pitch -82.43  1.778 deg

Under two degrees at every pose and under half a degree in four of five.

TWO FAULTS IN THE ANALYSIS, BOTH MINE, FOUND BEFORE THE RESULT WAS USED.

The first run of this comparison reported the filter disagreeing with
trigonometry by 16 and 25 degrees in the pitch poses. Both numbers were
artefacts.

One: --capture averaged roll and pitch arithmetically across the settled
window. Roll wraps at plus and minus 180 degrees, and the pitch poses
converge to +148 and +177, so the mean of values straddling that wrap is a
number no sample ever held.

Two: it then reported a difference of Euler angles as the filter's error.
Near a pitch of ninety degrees roll is ill-conditioned, and a small
orientation error becomes a large roll figure. Multiplying the reported
gaps by the cosine of the pitch brings 16.5 and 25.0 degrees back to 3.4
and 2.5, which is the size of the error the well-conditioned poses show.

Both are fixed: the program now reports the converged estimate rather than
a mean of angles, and an orientation error defined as the angle between the
gravity direction the filter's roll and pitch imply and the one measured.
That quantity has no singularity and means the same thing at every pose.

AND THE CRITERION AS WRITTEN CANNOT BE MET, for two separate reasons.

It asks for pitch and roll "within 3 degrees after a 90 degree rotation
about each axis". That measures the FIXTURE and not the filter. A board
propped on a free edge sits 2 to 12 degrees off the axis, as these four
captures show, and the filter reporting that honestly is the filter being
right. Meeting the criterion as written needs a machinist square or a
known-flat block, not better software.

And at the pitch pose it is ill-posed as well as impractical. With pitch
near minus ninety degrees, roll is not a determined quantity: the two
rotations very nearly share an axis, so "roll within 3 degrees" asks for a
tolerance on a number the geometry does not fix.

WHAT THE DATA DOES ESTABLISH, which is what the criterion was reaching for:
the filter's orientation agrees with trigonometry on the same samples to
better than two degrees at every pose tested, including both ninety degree
rotations, with no shared code between the two routes.

A restatement that tests the filter rather than the bench would be: the
orientation error against the measured gravity direction stays below 3
degrees at level and at a rotation near ninety degrees about each axis.
That is the number above, it is already met, and it is not changed by how
squarely the board was propped. Whether to amend the criterion is not a
decision this file makes.
