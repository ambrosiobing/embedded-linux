The LSM6DSV16X read from user space, with no kernel driver.
Wednesday 30 September 2026.

Host:   Raspberry Pi 3 Model B Plus Rev 1.3, hostname eplepi
Kernel: 6.18.50+rpt-rpi-v8, stock Raspberry Pi OS. No ST IIO drivers.
Part:   LSM6DSV16X at 0x6b, device tree node present, bound to nothing.

Started through i2c-dev alone:

root@eplepi:~# i2cset -y 1 0x6b 0x10 0x06      # CTRL1: 120 Hz, high performance
root@eplepi:~# i2cget -y 1 0x6b 0x10
0x06                                            # read back, accepted

REGISTER MAP CONFIRMED BY MEASUREMENT, not by assumption:

  0x0f  WHO_AM_I    reads 0x70
  0x10  CTRL1       written 0x06, reads back 0x06
  0x28  OUTX_L_A    little-endian word
  0x2a  OUTY_L_A
  0x2c  OUTZ_L_A

GRAVITY, board lying flat:

root@eplepi:~# i2cget -y 1 0x6b 0x2c w
0x4078

0x4078 is 16504. At the default two g full scale the sensitivity is
0.061 mg per count, so that is 1.007 g. Gravity, to within a percent, on
the axis normal to the board.

AT REST, typical sample from the loop:

  X 0xffb0   -80 counts    -4.9 mg
  Y 0x00d8  +216 counts   +13.2 mg
  Z 0x406f +16495 counts  +1.006 g

X and Y a few thousandths of a g, which is a board not perfectly level
plus the part's own zero-g offset. Z at one g. This is what a working
accelerometer at rest looks like and it cannot be faked by a stuck value.

UNDER DISTURBANCE, from the same loop:

  0x0174 0x0165 0x3ffc
  0xfe90 0x01cc 0x4078
  0x0238 0x0110 0x4220
  0xfed1 0xfc2c 0x3ea3
  0x0105 0xfd95 0x41f8

Excursions of several hundred to a thousand counts on X and Y, tens to
hundreds of milli-g, then a return to the resting values. Motion produces
a signal and its absence produces the resting triple.

WHAT THIS DEMONSTRATES. This chip has no driver in this kernel. Its
device tree node binds to nothing. /sys/bus/iio does not exist. And it is
delivering correct three-axis acceleration through eight bytes of i2c-dev
traffic. That is the argument Project 11 is built on, shown rather than
asserted: not every sensor needs a kernel driver to be useful.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DEMONSTRATE. Sample rate. Three i2cget calls per sample,
each a separate process, with a 0.2 s sleep, gives roughly five samples a
second against a part configured for 120. There are no timestamps. Nothing
here touches the hardware FIFO, the watermark interrupt on GPIO24, or any
of the buffered paths this project exists to compare. Those need the
drivers this kernel does not carry.

NOTE ON THE CAPTURE. The serial console emitted a block of binary garbage
partway through and then recovered. Project 1's bring-up document records
that this Renkforce cable is a PL2303HXA which "dropped its USB connection
twice during bring-up", and this is consistent with that. The readings
before and after the corruption are self-consistent, so the values stand,
but a capture intended as evidence should be taken over ssh rather than
over this cable.

CHARACTERISED. A capture of about 38 seconds at 0.2 s intervals, plotted
as three axes in g, with summary statistics.

  Axis   mean       min       max
  X     -0.001 g   -0.15 g   +0.25 g
  Y     +0.013 g   -0.67 g   +0.17 g
  Z     +1.007 g   +0.55 g   +1.21 g
  |a|    1.009 g

THE MAGNITUDE IS THE ACCEPTANCE CRITERION. The vector magnitude of a
stationary accelerometer must be one g, and it came out at 1.009 g, an
error of nine parts in a thousand. That is inside a MEMS part's normal
sensitivity tolerance and it needs no reference instrument, which is why
gravity is the right acceptance test for a bench with no calibration rig.

IT ALSO VALIDATES THE SCALE ASSUMPTION. The plot is titled "assumed two g
full scale, 16384 counts per g", and that assumption is now checked rather
than assumed: a wrong full-scale setting would have produced a magnitude
of about half a g or about two g, not 1.009. The register map and the
full-scale default are confirmed together by one number.

Note that the mean of the per-sample magnitudes, 1.009 g, is slightly
above the magnitude of the mean vector, 1.007 g. That is expected: noise
and the two disturbances add to the magnitude in both directions, and
averaging magnitudes cannot cancel them the way averaging components can.

TWO EVENTS ARE VISIBLE. A single sharp knock at about 24.5 s, seen as Y
falling to -0.67 g and Z dipping to 0.55 g in the same sample, and a burst
of oscillation between about 28 and 31 s on X, with Z reaching 1.21 g.

WHY THE EXTREMES ARE NOT THE PEAKS. At five samples a second, a knock
lasting a few milliseconds is caught by at most one sample and usually by
none. The -0.67 g on Y is one sample of a transient, not its maximum, and
the true peak of a tap on a desk is several g. So the min and max columns
above describe this capture rather than the events in it.

That is the clearest statement yet of what this project exists to fix. The
part was configured for 120 samples a second and delivered five, unevenly,
with no timestamps. The hardware FIFO and the watermark interrupt on
GPIO24 exist to take every one of the 120 with a timestamp attached, and
they need the drivers this kernel does not carry.
