A 142-fold reduction with sample delivery unchanged. Both sample counts are
exact multiples of the watermark, so the FIFO is batching correctly in both
cases, and the 3246 edges that disappeared were never carrying data.

WHY

INT1 is push-pull and stays asserted while the FIFO is above its watermark.
An edge-triggered request counts every transition in the bounce train that a
long unterminated wire produces around a fast transition. A level-sensitive
request asks whether the line is high when it looks, and a ring that settles
before the handler runs is invisible to it.

The wire was never the fault. The request was.

WHAT THE EARLIER MEASUREMENTS GOT RIGHT, AND WHAT THEY DID NOT ASK

The control of the same evening, with the wire unplugged at CN9 pin 6, gave
0.00 interrupts per second and ZERO samples. That was correct and nothing
here touches it: the edges were real and they arrived down the wire rather
than being picked up by the GPIO pin.

What nothing asked is whether a real edge and a USEFUL edge are the same
thing. A ring is real. It is on the wire. It is not information, and the way
to stop counting it was never to make the wire quieter.

THE CLUE THAT WAS PRINTED ON THE FIRST DAY AND NEVER INTERROGATED

The spurious factor was never constant: 23 to 51 on Thursday morning, then
30.1, 32.7 and 43.6 across the evening's runs. A reflection on a fixed length
of wire into a fixed impedance has no reason to vary by a factor of two
between runs at the same rate and watermark. A bounce train sampled by an
edge detector does, because whether a given ring crosses the threshold again
depends on where it is when the detector re-arms.

That number was read for four days as noise in the measurement. It was a
statement about the mechanism.

CRITERION 4, AND WHY ITS CONTRAST CLAUSE WAS RESTATED

  fewer than 8 interrupts per second at watermark 64    2.30     MET
  under 3 per cent reader CPU                           0.60 %   MET
  more than 400 per second at watermark 1              15.60     cannot be met

The third clause assumes one interrupt per sample, which is edge semantics. A
level-triggered line coalesces: row 4 is 4684 samples over 156 interrupts, 30
samples each, the handler draining the FIFO rather than batching failing.

The only way to reach 400 is to go back to rising edge, which is the
configuration that produced 326.90 per second at watermark 64 against an
expected 7.5. A contrast that can be satisfied only by the broken setup is
not a criterion.

Restated the same day, by Joseph, to a same-configuration ratio:

    at the same output data rate and the same trigger type, watermark 64
    produces at least 5 times fewer interrupts than watermark 1

Measured 15.60 / 2.30 = 6.8 times, which clears it, and it still tests what
the clause was for: that batching reduces interrupt load. The absolute halves
are unchanged because they do not depend on edge semantics.

The reader-CPU contrast, 1.50 per cent at watermark 1 against 0.60 at 64, is
real and survives both trigger types. It was considered as a replacement and
rejected: it is a second view of the reader-CPU half the criterion already
has, not a substitute for the interrupt claim.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW

  - that the wire is good. It shows the wire is adequate for a level-
    sensitive request. A shorter jumper might still be worth having and is no
    longer on the critical path for anything
  - anything about the LSM6DSO16IS at 0x6a, whose INT1 is on CN8 pin 6 and
    is not wired to the Pi at all
  - any measurement of edge timing. These are interrupt COUNTS; the ts sd
    column stays 0.124 us from driver-interpolated timestamps throughout and
    is not a latency measurement in any of the four rows
