WHY TWO SCANS WITH A WAIT, rather than one scan straight after the
trigger. kmemleak does not report an object younger than its minimum age,
and the first scan after an allocation commonly only marks candidates, so
a single immediate scan returning nothing would be a false negative and
not a result. buggy's own init message says the same thing: it tells the
reader to echo scan to see them.

FOUR INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATIONS that these are the objects the criterion
names, rather than any leak that happened to be present:

  the count     64, which is LEAK_OBJECTS in buggy.c
  the size      256, which is LEAK_OBJECT_SZ
  the contents  a5 repeated, because fault_leak memsets each block to 0xa5
  the stack     fault_leak and trigger_write, both in [buggy]

EXACTLY 64, NOT A MULTIPLE OF IT. The module had already been loaded and
unloaded ten times for criterion 9 before this run. Had its exit path
leaked, the count here would have been a multiple of 64. It is not, so
criterion 9's clean verdict is confirmed from a second direction.

This criterion is also the one unaffected by the dwc_otg log flood
recorded in module-cycle-2026-10-30.txt, because kmemleak is read from
debugfs rather than from the kernel ring buffer.
